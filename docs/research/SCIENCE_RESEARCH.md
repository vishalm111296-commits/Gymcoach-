# Sports Science & Training Research

A compilation of peer-reviewed sports science guidelines, training methodologies, and nutritional recommendations matching industry standards.

---

## 1. ACSM Resistance Training Guidelines (General & Hypertrophy)

The American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) outlines key parameters for developing muscular strength, hypertrophy, and local endurance.

### A. Frequency
* **Novice**: 2–3 days per week training the entire body.
* **Intermediate**: 3 days per week for total-body, or 4 days per week using a split routine (upper/lower).
* **Advanced**: 4–6 days per week, utilizing multi-split routines (push/pull/legs, bodypart splits).

### B. Intensity & Volume
* **Hypertrophy Focus**: 60%–80% of 1-Repetition Maximum (1RM) for 8–12 repetitions per set.
* **Volume**: 2–4 sets per exercise. Novices benefit from single/double set schemes, whereas trained individuals require higher volume (typically 10–20 working sets per muscle group weekly for optimal hypertrophy).
* **Rest Periods**: 2–3 minutes for multi-joint, compound exercises (squat, bench press); 1–2 minutes for single-joint isolation exercises.

## 2. Progressive Overload & Volume Tracking

Progressive overload is the gradual increase of stress placed upon the musculoskeletal system. GymCoach models volume and overload using three primary metrics:

### A. Volume Metrics
1. **Total Volume Load**: $\text{Sets} \times \text{Reps} \times \text{Weight}$. While simple, this can be skewed by high-rep, low-weight sets.
2. **Hard Sets (Working Sets)**: The count of sets completed within a close proximity to muscular failure (typically $\le 4$ Reps in Reserve). This is the primary metric for tracking hypertrophic stimulus.
3. **Indirect Set Attribution**: Secondary muscles (e.g., Triceps during a Bench Press) receive fractional credit ($0.5$ sets) to avoid over-calculating total weekly volume.

### B. Proximity to Failure (RPE & RIR)
* **Rating of Perceived Exertion (RPE)**: Borg's CR10 scale adapted for lifting. 10 is maximum effort, 9 is 1 rep remaining, 8 is 2 reps remaining.
* **Reps in Reserve (RIR)**: The inverse of RPE. $\text{RIR} = 10 - \text{RPE}$.
* **Overload Stimulus**: Research indicates that sets performed between $0\text{ RIR}$ and $4\text{ RIR}$ maximize motor unit recruitment and hypertrophic response. Training consistently at $0\text{ RIR}$ (absolute failure) increases fatigue exponentially without proportional gains, highlighting the utility of structured target RIR programming.

## 3. Protein & Macronutrient Recommendations

To support adaptation and recovery, dietary intake must align with energy expenditure and training intensity.

### A. Protein Intake
* **Daily Target**: $1.6\text{g}$ to $2.2\text{g}$ of protein per kilogram of body weight ($0.73\text{g}$ to $1.0\text{g}$ per pound) for active lifters.
* **Distribution**: $0.40\text{g/kg}$ to $0.55\text{g/kg}$ per meal, spread across 3–5 meals throughout the day.
* **Source Quality**: Essential Amino Acid (EAA) density, specifically leucine content ($\ge 3\text{g}$ per meal), drives muscle protein synthesis (MPS).

### B. Hydration & Carbohydrate Intake
* **Hydration**: $5$–$7\text{ mL/kg}$ of water at least 4 hours before training. During workout, hydrate to prevent $>2\%$ loss in body mass.
* **Carbohydrates**: $3$–$5\text{g/kg}$ per day for moderate lifting schedules to replenish glycogen stores. Consuming $30$–$60\text{g}$ of fast-acting carbs pre/intra workout sustains glucose availability.
