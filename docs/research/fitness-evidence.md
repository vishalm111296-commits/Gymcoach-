# Fitness Evidence: V-Taper Hypertrophy Program

Evidence-based research summary. Every claim carries a source (DOI/URL). Items without direct peer-reviewed support are explicitly labeled.

_Last verified: 2026-08-22_

---

## 1. Hypertrophy Fundamentals (Volume, Frequency, Rep Range, Proximity to Failure)

### Volume (sets per muscle per week)
- **ACSM Position Stand 2026** ("Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults: An Overview of Reviews", MSSE): synthesized **137 systematic reviews (>30,000 participants)**. Hypertrophy enhanced by **higher volume (≥10 sets/muscle group/week)** and eccentric overload. Meta-regression showed dose-response plateauing with diminishing returns beyond ~18–20 weekly sets for hypertrophy; minimum effective dose ≥2 sets/exercise.
  - Source: https://journals.lww.com/acsm-msse/fulltext/2026/04000/american_college_of_sports_medicine_position.21.aspx ; PubMed PMID 41843416
- **Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017**, J Sports Sci 35(11):1073–1082, meta-regression of 15 studies: graded dose-response; each additional weekly set ≈ +0.37% hypertrophy gain; categorical trend <5 sets (ES 0.307), 5–9 (0.378), **10+ (0.520)**.
  - DOI: 10.1080/02640414.2016.1210197
- Practical band supported by both sources: **10–20 hard sets/muscle/week** for trained lifters, starting lower for novices.

### Frequency
- **Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2016**, Sports Med 46(11):1689–1697, meta-analysis: training a muscle **2×/week superior to 1×/week** for hypertrophy in analyses where total weekly volume was NOT equated.
  - DOI: 10.1007/s40279-016-0543-8
- **Schoenfeld, Grgic & Krieger 2019**, J Sports Sci 37(11):1286–1295: when **weekly volume is equated**, higher frequencies show no significant additional hypertrophy benefit.
  - DOI: 10.1080/02640414.2018.1555906
- **Grgic, Schoenfeld & Latella 2019**, J Sci Med Sport 22(3):361–370 (review): same conclusion — frequency is primarily a volume-distribution tool.
  - DOI: 10.1016/j.jsams.2018.09.223
- **ACSM 2026**: low (1 d/wk) vs high (>5 d/wk) frequency did not affect hypertrophy when total volume equated; primary recommendation is RT at least 2 d/wk engaging all major muscle groups (source above).
- Synthesis: hit each muscle **≥2×/week** as the default distribution; total weekly volume is the operative variable.

### Rep ranges / load
- ACSM 2002 Position Stand, MSSE 34(2):364–380: hypertrophy loading **1–12 RM periodized, emphasis on the 6–12 RM zone**, rest 1–2 min between sets.
  - Source: https://journals.lww.com/acsm-msse/fulltext/2002/02000/progression_models_in_resistance_training_for.27.aspx
- ACSM 2026: hypertrophy was NOT significantly different across loads from **30% to 100% 1RM** (volume-equated) — light-to-heavy spectrum all viable with sufficient effort (source above).

### Proximity to failure (RIR/RPE)
- **Remmert, Robinson, Pelland, Refalo, Zourdos, Steele & Jukic 2024**, Sports Med 54(9):2209–2231, series of multilevel meta-regressions (140 hypertrophy effects / 26 studies): hypertrophy improves as sets are terminated **closer to failure** (marginal slope −0.019 to −0.023 per RIR; CI excludes null); strength gains are negligibly affected by proximity to failure. Authors flag exploratory nature and RIR-estimation uncertainty.
  - Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/ ; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382046673
- ACSM 2026: training **to momentary fatigue did not consistently impact outcomes** (i.e., failure not required) (source above).
- Synthesis: most working sets at **~0–3 RIR**; occasional failure acceptable on isolation/machine work; systematic failure training raises fatigue cost without clear hypertrophy premium.

## 2. Progressive Overload

- **Load-increment rule (ACSM)**: when the target rep count can be exceeded by 1–2 reps, increase load by **2–10%** (MSSE 34(2):364–380, source above).
- **ACSM 2026**: heavier loads (≥80% 1RM) drive strength; higher volumes drive hypertrophy; periodization type (linear/undulating/non-periodized) did **not** consistently impact outcomes (source above).
- Double progression, micro-loading, weekly progression models:
  - **No direct randomized trials comparing named progression algorithms (double progression vs %1RM linear vs micro-loading) were located** in this search (Europe PMC queries on progression/deload literature).
  - Status: practical conventions consistent with the ACSM load-increment rule above; treat specific algorithm choice as unverified preference, not evidence-based differential.

## 3. V-Taper Specific Training

### Lats (width)
- Grip width: **Andersen et al. 2014**, J Strength Cond Res 28(4):1135–1142, "Effects of grip width on muscle strength and activation in the lat pull-down."
  - DOI: 10.1097/jsc.0000000000000232
- Pulldown prime-mover excitation (latissimus, biceps): **Padovan et al. 2024**, J Hum Kinet 91:47–60, high-density sEMG front vs back pulldown.
  - DOI: 10.5114/jhk/185211 (open access: PMC11057623)
- Seated-row scapular-position effects on prime movers: **Padovan et al. 2025**, J Funct Morphol Kinesiol 11(1):6.
  - DOI: 10.3390/jfmk11010006
- **Gap**: No controlled trial was found comparing vertical-pulling vs horizontal-pulling **volume ratios** on lat hypertrophy. EMG studies exist (above); prescribing a specific ratio (e.g., 2:1) is convention, not evidence. Vertical pulls remain the exercises whose primary mover is the latissimus.

### Lateral deltoid (shoulder width)
- **Coratella et al. 2020**, Int J Environ Res Public Health 17(17):E6015, EMG analysis of lateral raise variants + frontal raise in competitive bodybuilders — deltoid regional activation varies by implement/variation.
  - DOI: 10.3390/ijerph17176015 (open access: PMC7503819)
- Overhead press variants (front/back, barbell/machine) EMG: **Coratella et al. 2022**, Front Physiol 13:825880.
  - DOI: 10.3389/fphys.2022.825880 (open access: PMC9354811)
- Upright row grip-width EMG (deltoid/trapezius activation): **McAllister et al. 2013**, J Strength Cond Res 27(1):181–187.
  - DOI: 10.1519/jsc.0b013e31824f23ad
- Direct lateral-raise volume beyond pressing: **no trial isolating lateral deltoid growth from compound-only vs compound+isolation was found** → gap. EMG supports isolation exercises as dedicated deltoid stimuli (sources above).

### Upper chest (clavicular pectoralis)
- **Albarello et al. 2022**, J Electromyogr Kinesiol 67:102722: bench inclination produces **non-uniform pectoralis excitation leading to uneven regional cross-sectional area changes** measured by panoramic ultrasound — region-specific growth follows region-specific excitation.
  - DOI: 10.1016/j.jelekin.2022.102722
- Horizontal vs incline bench press training adaptations (untrained men, neuromuscular): **Chaves et al. 2020**, Int J Exerc Sci 13(6):859–872.
  - DOI: 10.70252/fdnb1158 (PMC7449336)
- Widely-cited incline-angle EMG studies (e.g., 30° optimum claims) were **not independently verified in this search pass** — do not encode a specific "optimal angle" constant without further verification.

### Rear deltoid
- Posterior deltoid is measured as a prime/co-mover across rowing, upright-row, and overhead-press EMG studies cited above (McAllister 2013; Coratella 2022; Padovan 2025).
- Role in perceived shoulder width: **aesthetic inference only — no study quantifies rear-delt size vs shoulder-width appearance** → unverified claim category.

## 4. Recovery Science

- MPS/recovery window: **Damas, Libardi & Ugrinowitsch 2018**, Eur J Appl Physiol 118(3):485–500 (review): resistance-training hypertrophy relates to elevated myofibrillar protein synthesis **after attenuation of muscle damage**; early-session damage inflates MPS independent of growth stimulus — supports ~48h spacing between sessions for the same muscle in early training.
  - DOI: 10.1007/s00421-017-3792-9
- Related longitudinal evidence: **Damas et al. 2016**, J Physiol 594(18):5209–5222.
  - DOI: 10.1113/jp272472
- Deloads (direct RCT evidence now exists):
  - **Coleman et al. 2024**, PeerJ 12:e16777 — RCT on a one-week deload during supervised RT ("Gaining more from doing less?"): deload did not enhance muscular adaptations vs continuous training.
    - DOI: 10.7717/peerj.16777 (open access: PMC10809978)
  - **Pancar et al. 2026**, Sci Rep 16:10299 — within-subject randomized design on deload periods in untrained young men.
    - DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-40612-5 (open access: PMC13031491)
  - Practice surveys: De Marco 2024 JSCR 38(12):2099–2106 (DOI: 10.1519/jsc.0000000000004932); Rogerson 2024 Sports Med Open 10:26 (DOI: 10.1186/s40798-024-00691-y).
- Overtraining-syndrome diagnostic signs: **not verified against a specific source in this pass** → gap (see Gaps).

## 5. Training Splits

- **Ramos-Campo et al. 2024**, J Strength Cond Res 38(7):1330–1340, "Efficacy of Split Versus Full-Body Resistance Training on Strength and Muscle Growth: A Systematic Review With Meta-Analysis."
  - DOI: 10.1519/jsc.0000000000004774
- Direct RCTs comparing splits vs full-body (volume considerations vary):
  - Pedersen et al. 2022, BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil 14:87 — split-body vs full-body in untrained women. DOI: 10.1186/s13102-022-00481-7
  - Evangelista et al. 2021, Einstein (Sao Paulo) 19:eAO5781. DOI: 10.31744/einstein_journal/2021ao5781
  - Bartolomei et al. 2021, JSCR 35(6):1520–1526 — total body vs split in trained men. DOI: 10.1519/jsc.0000000000003573
  - Carneiro et al. 2024, Eur J Sport Sci 24(6):846–854 — full-body produced greater fat-mass loss than split in well-trained males. DOI: 10.1002/ejsc.12104
- Consistency with frequency literature (Section 1): split choice is a **volume-delivery scheduling decision**; no split demonstrates inherent hypertrophy superiority. Upper/lower or PPL both satisfy the ≥2×/muscle/week criterion.

## 6. Range of Motion

- **Pallarés et al. 2021**, Scand J Med Sci Sports 31(10):1866–1881, SR/meta-analysis: **full ROM superior to partial ROM** for resistance-training adaptations (hypertrophy, strength, endurance).
  - DOI: 10.1111/sms.14006
- **Schoenfeld & Grgic 2020**, SAGE Open Med 8:2050312120901559, SR: full ROM at least comparable to partial; findings nuanced by joint angle and muscle length.
  - DOI: 10.1177/2050312120901559 (open access: PMC6977096)
- Full vs equalized partial ROM on muscle architecture: **Valamatos et al. 2018**, Eur J Appl Physiol 118(9):1969–1983.
  - DOI: 10.1007/s00421-018-3932-x
- Lengthened-position emphasis (long-length partials literature) is an active research area; individual lengthened-partial RCTs were **not individually verified here** → treat "lengthened-biased ROM" as promising-but-unverified until primary studies are checked.
- Default rule supported: **full ROM through the stretch-position-biased range**; shortened-only partials lack support.

## 7. Tempo / Time Under Tension

- **Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2015**, Sports Med 45(4):577–585, SR/meta-analysis on repetition duration: moderate durations (~2–8 s per rep) produce the greatest hypertrophic outcomes; very slow durations inferior.
  - DOI: 10.1007/s40279-015-0304-0
- Controlled vs self-selected tempo RCT: **Chaves et al. 2020**, PeerJ 8:e8697 — controlled tempo conferred **no significant advantage** over self-selected repetition duration for muscle mass/strength in untrained men.
  - DOI: 10.7717/peerj.8697 (open access: PMC7061915)
- Motor-unit/fibre activation unaffected by load and duration when sets reach task failure: **Morton et al. 2019**, J Physiol 597(17):4601–4613.
  - DOI: 10.1113/jp278056
- ACSM 2026: time under tension, and fast (0.5 s) vs slow (8 s) repetition speed comparisons, did not consistently impact outcomes (source above).
- Synthesis: prescribe a loose guardrail (controlled eccentric, no bouncing, ~1–3 s concentric) rather than rigid tempo codes; TUT targets add complexity without evidenced benefit.

## Unverified Claims (do NOT encode as fact)

1. Specific vertical:horizontal pull volume ratios (e.g., 2:1) for lat width.
2. A single "optimal" incline angle (e.g., exactly 30°) for upper chest.
3. Rear-delt size driving perceived shoulder width.
4. Named progression algorithms (double progression, micro-loading) outperforming load-% schemes.
5. Mandatory periodic deloads improving hypertrophy outcomes (current RCT evidence shows no added benefit; Coleman 2024).
6. Spot-reduction/waist-narrowing via abdominal exercise (not searched here; classical spot-reduction literature does not support it — verify before claiming).

## Gaps / Unknowns

- Overtraining-syndrome diagnostic criteria: needs citation pass (candidates exist, e.g., Kreher & Schwartz, EJAP — unverified in this pass).
- Lateral deltoid: isolation-vs-compound long-term hypertrophy trials absent.
- Lengthened-partial ROM primary RCTs: pending individual verification.
- Muscle-group-specific volume tolerance differences (e.g., side delts tolerating higher volumes): mechanistic plausibility only.
